📚 Reg's Painting Library

46 years of techniques, mistakes, and things that actually work. No fluff.

Core Painting Techniques

Basecoating

Thin paint to milk consistency. Two thin coats beat one thick coat every time. Thick paint fills in detail and you'll never get it back. Use a medium brush and work methodically. Prime first — grey for dark colours, white for bright colours, black for a grim/gritty look.

Washing / Shading

Washes flow into recesses and create instant shadow. Citadel Agrax Earthshade, Nuln Oil, and Reikland Fleshshade are workhorses. Army Painter Dark Tone and Strong Tone are excellent value. Apply over a sealed basecoat, let gravity do the work. Don't glob it on.

Drybrushing

Load a stiff brush, wipe almost all paint off on a tissue, then lightly drag across raised surfaces. Picks out edges and texture instantly. Perfect for fur, hair, chainmail, stone, wood. Works best on textured surfaces. Don't skip this step — it transforms a flat model.

Edge Highlighting

The signature of a display piece. Pick up a lighter mix of your colour and run a fine brush along raised edges. Start with a broader highlight, then a sharper bright line at corners. Needs a steady hand and patience. The single skill that separates good from great.

Glazing

Very thin, transparent paint washes over an area to tint without covering. Use a glazing medium or heavily thin with water + flow improver. Builds up colour gradually, adds OSL (object source lighting) effects, rescues colours that went wrong. A glaze of red over orange deepens it beautifully.

Basing

The base finishes the model. A brilliant paintjob on a plain black base looks unfinished. Texture paste, PVA + sand, static grass, tufts — all cheap and transformative. Match the base to the world your model inhabits. Snow? Urban rubble? Jungle floor? The base tells the story.

Paint Ranges — What Reg Actually Uses

Citadel (Games Workshop)

The industry standard. Expensive but consistent. The Contrast/Shade/Layer/Base system works. Agrax Earthshade is the most useful paint ever made. Contrast paints are genuinely brilliant for batch painting. Buy where they're available — the pots dry out if you don't seal them properly.

Vallejo

Superior dropper bottles — no dried paint lids. Model Colour for general use. Game Colour slightly more vibrant for fantasy. Model Air pre-thinned for airbrush. The best value-per-ml in the hobby. Metallics are outstanding. Reg's personal recommendation for anyone starting out.

Army Painter

Speedpaints are the Contrast competitor — quick and effective. Warpaints are solid workhorse paints. The dip (quick shade) is legendary for batch painting armies fast. Good for beginners who want results without faff. Fanatic range updated the formula significantly.

Scale75 / Infinity

High-end, slightly grittier finish ideal for display pieces. Excellent for realistic skin tones and non-metallic metals (NMM). More demanding to use but the results at competition level are hard to beat. Not for beginners — get the basics down first.

AK Interactive / MIG

Scale modelling crossover paints. Excellent weathering products — rust effects, oil paints, pigments. If you're painting vehicles or terrain, these are essential. AK real metal paints are outstanding.

Scales, Manufacturers & Ranges

28mm / 32mm — The Mainstream

Warhammer 40K, Age of Sigmar, Dungeons & Dragons, most tabletop RPG figures. Games Workshop dominates but Privateer Press, Mantic, Warlord, Reaper, and hundreds of boutique makers all produce here. Most tutorials assume this scale.

15mm & 10mm — Historical Wargaming

Flames of War (15mm WWII), Pendraken (10mm), Baccus (6mm). The scale of serious historical wargamers. Technically demanding — less detail but you're painting hundreds. Drybrushing and washes do most of the heavy lifting.

54mm & 75mm — Display Pieces

Andrea Miniatures, Pegaso, Romeo Models. Not for gaming — for competition and display. Requires advanced techniques: NMM, OSL, fine blending. These are the pieces you see at Golden Demon and Salute. The full-size canvas of the hobby.

Key UK Manufacturers

Games Workshop (Nottingham) — industry giant. Warlord Games (Nottingham) — historical and Beyond the Gates of Antares. North Star Military Figures — Osprey games, Frostgrave. Perry Miniatures — exceptional historical sculpts by the Perry twins. Victrix — hard plastic historicals. Footsore Miniatures — Dark Ages specialists.

The Resin Revolution

3D printing and resin casting have changed everything. Kickstarter boutique ranges from Corvus Belli, Raging Heroes, Creature Caster. Independent sculptors selling STLs directly. Wash resin in IPA before painting — always. Prime heavily — resin doesn't take paint as well as metal.

❓ Common Questions

What Reg gets asked at every show. With straight answers.

How do I stop getting brush strokes in my basecoat?+
Thin your paint. If you can't see through it on your palette it's too thick. Add water a drop at a time until it flows smoothly. Two thin coats always beat one thick coat. Use a medium brush, not a big one — you have more control. Let each coat fully dry before the next. Patience is the most underrated painting skill.
What's the best way to start as a complete beginner?+
Buy one cheap box of plastics (Warhammer Starter Set, Oathmark, Frostgrave soldiers). Get Citadel or Army Painter starter paint sets. Watch YouTube — Painting Buddha, Miniac, Luke's APS, and Squidmar are all excellent. Don't buy 30 pots of paint you don't need. Learn basecoat, wash, drybrush first — that alone gets you 80% of the way there. Don't expect your first model to be brilliant. Reg's first model looked like it was painted by a boot.
Should I prime? Which primer?+
Yes. Always prime. Without it, paint doesn't adhere properly and will chip and flake. For dark/gritty armies: black or grey primer. For bright colours or NMM: white. For a midtone shortcut: grey. Spray cans (Army Painter, Citadel) are fastest. Brush-on primer works fine for small batches. Vallejo primer through an airbrush gives the finest coat. Never prime in very cold or humid conditions — the finish will go grainy.
What brushes do I actually need?+
Three brushes to start: a size 2 for basecoating, a size 1 for general work, and a fine detail brush (0 or 00) for eyes and small work. Kolinsky sable is the gold standard — Raphael 8404, Winsor & Newton Series 7. Synthetic brushes wear faster but are fine for washing and drybrushing (which destroys brushes). Keep detail brushes for detail only. Clean brushes immediately after use — dried paint kills them.
How do I paint eyes on 28mm figures?+
The honest answer: most painters cheat, and that's fine. Fill the eye socket with a dark wash, then add a small dot of white, then an even smaller dot of dark (brown or black) in the centre. The brain fills in the rest. For beginners, leaving eyes as darkened recesses reads fine at tabletop distance. Reserve the proper two-highlight eye technique for display pieces or heroes. If you go wrong, paint over and start again — eyes take practice.
How do I strip paint off a model I've ruined?+
Dettol (brown, concentrated) is the UK standard. Soak in Dettol for a few hours, scrub with an old toothbrush under running water. Resin: IPA (isopropyl alcohol) from a chemist — soak for 30 mins max, resin can get brittle. Plastic: Dettol only, not IPA — it melts plastic. Metal: almost anything works, even nail polish remover. Don't use nail polish remover on plastic. Ever.
How do I batch paint an army efficiently?+
Assembly-line paint in groups of 10-15. One colour across the whole batch before moving to the next. Prime everything. Basecoat in bulk. Wash everything in one pass. Drybrush all at once. Final details on each individually. Accept that batch-painted models are "good enough at arm's length" — not display pieces. The goal is a painted army on the table, not a competition entry. Done is better than perfect.
Do I need a varnish/sealer?+
Yes, for gaming models — absolutely. Matt varnish (Testors Dullcote, Army Painter Anti-Shine) protects the paintjob from handling. Games chew through unvarnished models fast. For display pieces, skip the varnish or use gloss only on gems and eyes to make them pop. Never spray varnish in cold or humid conditions — it goes cloudy ("frosting") and ruins everything. Brush-on varnish is safer if you're nervous about the weather.

Raphael 8404 or Winsor & Newton Series 7. Buy a size 0, 1, and 2. They hold a point, hold paint, and last years if you look after them. More important than any paint you buy.

Vallejo Model Colour Starter Set

72 bottles in dropper format. Saves you hunting for individual colours. Vallejo metallics especially are superb. The best value paint purchase you can make.

Wet Palette

Keeps paint workable for hours. Masterson Sta-Wet, or make your own with a Tupperware lid, kitchen sponge, and baking paper. Game-changing for blending.

Spray Primer

Army Painter Matt Black or Grey primer. Citadel equivalent. Light even coats from 30cm. Never prime in the cold or damp. Foundation of every paintjob.
